04

The Secret

Page turning is one of the hardest problems in robotics

Pages are thin, flexible, and unpredictable. They change shape during manipulation. They require precise force control and real-time vision feedback. This is deformable object manipulation — the unsolved frontier of robotics.

Solved

Rigid Manipulation

Pick up a can. Move a box. Stack blocks.
Every major robotics lab, every foundation model.

Unsolved — We’re here

Deformable Manipulation

Pages. Fabric. Food. Cables. Tissue.
Scattered across PhD labs. No one has productized it.

More bedtime stories → Better deformable models → Transfer to fabric, food, surgery → Platform revenue ↻

The arm is open-source. The deformable intelligence isn’t.
Every page turn generates labeled trajectories on non-rigid materials — a dataset no one else is collecting.

How It Works

See → Act → Speak → Repeat

See

Claude Vision assesses the scene in under 20 tokens. Book open, closed, done? Content page or blank? The robot decides what to do next.

Act

Separately trained motor policies — open, turn, close — each learned from demonstration. Precise, gentle, repeatable.

Speak

Text streamed to ElevenLabs before the page is fully processed. Sub-second latency. Voice cloning makes it personal.
